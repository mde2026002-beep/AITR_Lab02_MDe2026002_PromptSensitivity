## Prompt 14: Interpretation, by research question

### RQ1: Do conclusions change across equivalent prompts? **Yes, for most abstracts (H1 supported).**

**Direct observations:**

| | Qwen2.5-0.5B | Llama-3.2-1B |
|---|---|---|
| Flip rate within the paraphrase family | **63.4%** [59.2, 67.6] | **97.6%** [96.2, 99.0] |
| Errica sensitivity | 34.6% | 52.9% |
| Fleiss κ | 0.11 | −0.10 |

- 308 of 500 abstracts (61.6%) change conclusion under paraphrase **for both models**.
- Two analysts who each use one (different) fully specified prompt reach the same conclusion on only **52.0%** (Qwen) or **60.7%** (Llama) of abstracts.
- **Accuracy hides this.** Llama's P0–P3 accuracies lie within 53.8–56.4%. Yet P0 answers "yes" for 98.2% of abstracts while P2 answers "no" for 65.8%. Two prompts with near-identical accuracy reach opposite conclusions for most studies. This reproduces Errica et al.'s point (aggregate accuracy hides instance-level sensitivity) at the level of research conclusions.
- **Mechanism (INFERRED):** the paraphrases shift each model's default label (its prior) rather than its reading of the evidence. The label-share figure shows each prompt pushing the model towards one dominant answer: "maybe" for Qwen P1/P3/P4, "no" for Llama P2/P4.
- **Alternative explanation:** weak models (B1 ≈ majority class) have little evidence-driven signal, so any prior shift flips them. Larger models may be more stable, but this is untested here.

### RQ2: Which kind of change matters? **It depends on the model, and the lowest flip rates are degenerate (H2 not supported).**

**Flip rate by family (raw):**

| Family | Qwen2.5-0.5B | Llama-3.2-1B |
|---|---|---|
| Format | **94.6%** | 3.0% |
| Paraphrase | 63.4% | **97.6%** |
| Underspecified | 7.2% | 81.0% |

- All CIs within a model are non-overlapping.
- **Every low flip rate coincides with collapse onto one label:**
  - Qwen, underspecified: 97.4% of predictions are "yes".
  - Llama, format: 99.0% are "yes".
- H2 predicted that underspecified prompts would be the least stable. Instead, underspecified prompts were *more* stable than fully specified paraphrases for both models. They were also the most accurate for Llama (M5 has the best Llama macro-F1, 43.3).
- **Caveat:** label mass under underspecified prompts is low (0.00–0.38), so the model would not naturally answer with a bare label. Restricted scoring forces a choice, and this protocol choice affects the comparison (Hua et al. 2025).

**Prompt vs sampling variance:**

| Pairwise disagreement | Qwen | Llama |
|---|---|---|
| Between paraphrases (greedy) | 34.6% | 52.9% |
| Within one prompt, sampling at T = 0.7 | 31.6% | 46.0% |
| Within one prompt, sampling at T = 1.0 | 41.7% | 53.8% |
| Paraphrases sampled at T = 1.0 | 49.8% | 61.2% |

- Rewording a prompt changes conclusions about as much as resampling at T = 0.7–1.0, and the two effects add.
- Unlike sampling noise, prompt effects are **systematic**: repeating the same prompt does not average them away.

**Model rankings flip:**
- Llama has the higher macro-F1 under 9 prompts and Qwen under 5.
- 49.5% of prompt pairs disagree about which model is better. This reproduces Mizrahi et al.'s ranking instability on a research-conclusion task.

### RQ3: Do the mitigations help? **Calibration: no. Ensembling: yes for Llama, mixed for Qwen (H3 not supported).**

**Contextual calibration (CC):**
- **Qwen:** macro-F1 24.6 vs 24.5 (B1). Accuracy 55.2, identical to MAJ, with 98.4% "yes".
- **Llama:** accuracy 11.4 and macro-F1 7.1 (p < 0.001 vs B1), with 99.6% "maybe".
- **Stability:**
  - Qwen's paraphrase flip rate fell from 63.4% to 15.6%, but only by collapsing onto "yes".
  - Llama's did not improve (96.8%), and its two-analyst agreement fell (k = 1: 60.7% → 43.6%).
- **Mechanism (VERIFIED from the content-free distributions):**
  - Qwen answers "maybe" to empty input (p̂_cf for P0 = 0.05 / 0.40 / 0.55). The calibration weights 1/p̂_cf are therefore about 20 for "yes" against 2.5 for "no" and 1.8 for "maybe".
  - Llama answers "no" (0.27 / 0.73 / 0.003). Its weights are about 3.7 for "yes", 1.4 for "no" and **about 330 for "maybe"**.
- **Why (INFERRED):** CC assumes the answer to an empty input is pure bias. For a task with an "insufficient evidence" label, the empty input has a *correct* answer, so CC penalises correct behaviour. This qualifies Zhao et al. 2021 rather than contradicting it: their tasks had no abstention class and used base models.

**Ensembles:**
- **Llama:**
  - ENS-para: macro-F1 37.3 vs 25.7; accuracy 59.8 vs 55.8, McNemar p = 0.027.
  - ENS-all: accuracy 60.4, p = 0.001.
  - Two-analyst agreement rises from 60.7% (k = 1) to 93.4% (k = 4).
  - This meets the "helps" decision rule.
- **Qwen:**
  - ENS-para *hurts* (macro-F1 16.8, accuracy 20.2), because the paraphrases share a "maybe" bias that averaging amplifies.
  - ENS-all helps macro-F1 (29.4) by mixing prompts with opposite biases.
  - Agreement rises from 52.0% to 68.9%.
- **Lesson (INFERRED):** ensembling only helps when the prompt biases are diverse. Ensembling paraphrases that share a bias reinforces it.

**Dev-selected prompt (B2):**
- B2 gives the best macro-F1 for both models (Qwen F2: 40.5; Llama P2: 39.1).
- The winning prompt is model-specific: F2 scores 23.7 on Llama and P2 scores 29.0 on Qwen.
- For Llama, B2's accuracy does not differ from B1 (p = 0.59).

### Deeper analyses (failure and error analysis)

- **Per-class:** under P0, Qwen recalls 1.2% of "no" studies and Llama 3.0%. Llama recalls 0% of "maybe". The canonical prompt almost never concludes "no", which would bias a systematic review towards positive findings (INFERRED practical risk).
- **Confidence is not a safety signal:** for Qwen, abstracts in the *highest*-confidence quartile under P0 flip most often (82.4%, vs 50.4–64.8% in the other quartiles).
- **Cost:**
  - Measured 0.52 s per pass (Qwen) and 0.95 s per pass (Llama).
  - Total scoring time: 71 min and 151 min.
  - ENS-all costs 14× B1. Two-analyst agreement shows that most of the gain for Llama comes by k = 3 (89.4%).
- **Qualitative example** (Llama, pubid 20537205, "Is halofantrine ototoxic?", gold yes):
  - P0, P3 and F1–F4 say yes; P1 and P4 say maybe; P2 says no.
  - The authors' conclusion is "can be considered an ototoxic drug".
  - The same evidence yields all three conclusions depending on wording.

### Supported, contradicted, unresolved

- **H1** (≥ 10% flips): supported for both models.
- **H2** (underspecification is least stable): contradicted for both models.
- **H3** (calibration improves stability): contradicted. For Qwen, the stability gain is degenerate.
- **Unresolved:**
  - whether larger models behave differently
  - whether free-text generation (with parsing) shows more or less instability
  - whether CC computed only on the yes/no subset avoids the abstention problem

### Recommended follow-ups (ranked by cost and information gain)

1. Re-run the frozen protocol for Qwen2.5-1.5B (about 4 h on the same machine). This tests whether the instability shrinks with scale.
2. CC variant: estimate bias with content-free inputs only over {yes, no}. This tests the abstention mechanism directly (no new scoring needed).
3. Free-text generation for 100 abstracts × 5 paraphrases with a recorded parser. This measures how much parsing changes the measured sensitivity (Hua et al. 2025).
